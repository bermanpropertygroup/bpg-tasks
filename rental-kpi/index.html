Data coverage & how to read this

Actuals (collected, expenses, net income, net cash) come from each entity’s QuickBooks books for Jan–Sep 2026, cash basis: live QBO P&L BM + transaction detail by customer plus per-property Statements of Cash Flows, pushed to Gmail as scheduled TYTLM reports. Properties are customers and units are sub-customers in QBO, which is what makes the per-property split possible. Entity totals reconcile to the penny against each company P&L. Expected figures — GSR, proforma expenses, NOI, CF, DSCR — come from the SREO workbook and are kept alongside the actuals rather than replaced, so model-vs-reality gaps stay visible. Market bands refresh from public listing data with each run.

Reading the actuals honestly

Cash-basis monthly figures are lumpy by nature: Jan–Feb activity for several properties was journaled in February, tenants pay in catch-up batches, and insurance/tax entries land in single months. Judge properties on YTD and trend, not any one month. Net income is after mortgage interest but before principal; net cash is after principal, escrow, and capex. One-time items (marked †) — the February 800 Paris refinance, 409 Carteret and 137 Old Jericho capex — distort their periods and are called out where they appear.

Excluded from this view

1827 Ribaut Rd (self-storage — has its own investor reporting) and 614 Prince St (vacant lot, no income).

Definitions

GSR = gross scheduled rent. NOI = GSR − vacancy loss − operating expenses (before debt service). Cash flow = NOI − principal & interest. YTD = Jan–Sep 2026 (9 closed months). September is closed for Ember (books thru 10/7), 2203 (thru 10/2) and BPG (thru 10/9). September is PARTIAL (†) for Viktor: 609 Carteret and 409 Carteret Sep mortgage interest / principal are not yet posted, so Viktor Sep NI, Actual CF and actual DSCR are not final. Viktor Jan–Aug is the final basis.

DSCR appears twice, and the two mean different things. DSCR proforma is the SREO model: budgeted NOI ÷ scheduled debt service — what the property should do in a normal year. DSCR actual is what it did: collected income − operating expenses, divided by interest plus principal actually paid Jan–Sep (Viktor Sep partial). A property can model well and perform badly (153 Williams: 2.26 proforma vs 0.88 actual, because it sat vacant January and February), or the reverse. Where the two diverge, the actual is the one to act on and the gap is the thing worth explaining.
